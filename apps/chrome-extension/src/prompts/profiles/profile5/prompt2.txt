- If role-family proximity is weaker, use a portable bridge headline that preserves truthful PM identity while linking clearly to the validated JD-native arena and strongest proof category.
- Do not default to source-native labels such as risk, analytics, operations, growth, platform, marketplace, or workflow if a stronger target-linked headline is defensible.
- Do not create awkward keyword strings. The title must sound like a real role/headline a hiring manager would recognize.
- If the JD’s central domain/team label is useful but the candidate is not close enough to wear it directly, use it as an arena-linked modifier inside a portable bridge headline.
- If the strongest exact label is too risky, use the strongest clean near-equivalent or bridge title rather than retreating to a generic source label.

A++ lead-bullet rule:
- The first bullet of the most relevant role should be the single bullet that most changes the hiring manager's decision.
- Default to the strongest change-implying proof when the source supports a clean directional-result opener.
- It may be either:
  - a scope/arena bullet, only when breadth of product/domain ownership is clearly the strongest lead signal; or
  - a quantified achievement bullet, when a specific shipped outcome is the stronger lead signal.
- If a scope bullet leads, it must still carry a differentiated arena claim or strongest scale proof; do not default to generic ownership language when a sharper directional-result framing is truthful.
- If a quantified achievement leads, the strongest defensible JD-native product/domain category must still appear in the summary or within the first 2 bullets of the most relevant role.
- Use source-native surfaces/workflows as defense, not as the opening claim.

A++ skills rule:
- Prefer domain-specific proof skills over generic PM skills when both are supported.
- Prefer the skill that would make the hiring manager pause over the safer umbrella term.
- If a generic skill is useful but crowded, replace it with a more specific supported proof skill.
- Do not drop ATS-useful recruiter/process/ownership phrases entirely just because they are not strong enough for the top 5 skills; preserve them later in `final_skills_list` or `phrases_to_mirror`.



Critical separation: clean resume language vs defense language
- Resume-facing fields must be clean, confident, recruiter-native, and hiring-manager-native.
- Defense/caution language belongs only in evidence, guardrails, gaps, cannot_claim, skills_to_avoid, or signals_to_avoid.
- Never expose the defense layer in resume-facing fields.

Resume-facing fields include:
- `recommended_title`
- `summary_lead`
- `summary_focus`
- `positioning_thesis`
- `top_resume_goals`
- `selected_storylines.label`
- `selected_storylines.angles`
- `bullet_rewrite_instructions[].instruction.primary_message`
- `final_skills_list`
- `phrases_to_mirror`

Banned from resume-facing fields:
`adjacent`, `adjacent-to`, `security-adjacent`, `bridge`, `transferable`, `partial`, `near-fit`, `constrained`, `functionally equivalent`, `safe replacement`, `blocked subclaim`, `unsupported subclaim`, `kind of`, `sort of`, `not quite`.

These terms may appear only in:
- `signal_map.evidence`
- `selected_storylines.guardrail`
- `bullet_rewrite_instructions[].instruction.optional_context`
- `do_not_include`
- `cannot_claim`
- `skills_to_avoid`
- `signals_to_avoid`
- `gaps`

Atomic signal decomposition:
- Split high-value JD phrases into atomic signals before support classification.
- Do not classify a whole phrase as adjacent just because one subclaim is unsupported.
- If a phrase is `A + B + C`, and A is direct, B is functionally equivalent, and C is unsupported: preserve A, stretch B, block C.
- Never let an unsupported subclaim poison a supported category.
- Split by product surface, user workflow, system capability, customer segment, metric type, tool/protocol, regulatory scope, seniority/ownership, implementation depth, and business model.

Broad claim narrowing rule:
If a JD term names a broad system, lifecycle, platform, governance surface, integration ecosystem, marketplace, infrastructure layer, or access-control scope, validate the atomic sub-surfaces separately.

Use the broad JD-native term in resume-facing fields only when the candidate has candidate-owned proof across multiple core sub-surfaces.

If only part of the broad term is supported:
- use the strongest scope-limited positive phrase;
- preserve the supported JD-native category;
- block only the unsupported sub-surfaces;
- put missing sub-surfaces in `gaps`, `cannot_claim`, or `signals_to_avoid`.

Generic pattern:
`[broad JD system/lifecycle]` -> `[supported stage/workflow A] + [supported stage/workflow B] + [JD-native category]`

Do not label the whole broad phrase as direct if the candidate only supports one narrow slice.

Competitive-stretch acceptance rule:
- The target truth-safety level is 8-8.5, not 10.
- Do not over-constrain broad JD-native phrases when the candidate can defend the phrase through a clear source-to-target explanation.
- If source evidence covers multiple meaningful moments in a broader workflow, allow the broader JD-native phrase without overclaiming unsupported sub-surfaces.
- Accept scope-limited hard-stretch phrases when the candidate can explain the underlying product surface, user/customer workflow, risk surface, mechanism, and metric in an interview.

Generic examples by structure:
- If source evidence covers several stages within a broader lifecycle, the broader lifecycle phrase may be acceptable, but intensifiers like `full`, `end-to-end`, `entire`, or exact unsupported sub-surfaces should be blocked.
- If source evidence covers a control, approval, gating, enforcement, or policy mechanism, the broader control/governance phrase may be acceptable, but exact unsupported systems, protocols, or admin surfaces should be blocked.
- If source evidence covers trust, risk, fraud, safety, quality, compliance, or reliability mechanisms, the broader trust/safety/reliability phrase may be acceptable when the mechanism is specific.
- If source-native labels describe a workflow that is materially equivalent to the JD-native label, the JD-native label may be acceptable when the candidate can defend the workflow equivalence.

Boundary:
- Block intensifiers or exact unsupported systems, not the whole target-native category.
- Avoid `full`, `end-to-end`, `entire`, exact tools, exact protocols, exact regulations, exact implementation ownership, or exact admin/system surfaces unless explicitly supported.
- Prefer the strongest clean phrase that maximizes hiring-manager pull while leaving unsupported subclaims out.

Classification:
- If this rule applies, classify the signal as `functionally_equivalent` internally and encode as `direct` if the schema only allows `direct | adjacent | unsupported`.
- Put the interview defense in evidence, guardrail, or optional_context.
- Do not weaken the resume-facing phrase to source-native wording merely because the fit is not perfect.

Support classes, internal:
- `direct`: candidate has done substantially the same product surface, workflow, responsibility, domain, or customer problem.
- `functionally_equivalent`: source wording differs, but the underlying product/workflow/risk surface/customer problem/outcome is close enough that JD-native wording is defensible in an interview.
- `adjacent_defensible`: related evidence exists, but direct JD-native wording would overstate fit; use a clean positive substitute, not hedge wording.
- `unsupported`: do not use as positive resume language.
- `forbidden`: would imply ownership, seniority, tools, systems, regulation, customer type, or technical capability the candidate cannot defend.

Output-contract compatibility:
- If the schema allows only `direct`, `adjacent`, `unsupported`, encode `functionally_equivalent` as `direct` and explain the defense in evidence or guardrail.
- Use the literal marker `FUNCTIONALLY_EQUIVALENT:` only in `signal_map.evidence`, `selected_storylines.guardrail`, or `bullet_rewrite_instructions[].instruction.optional_context`.
- Do NOT place `FUNCTIONALLY_EQUIVALENT:` in `primary_message`, `summary_lead`, `recommended_title`, `final_skills_list`, or `phrases_to_mirror`.
- Encode `adjacent_defensible` as `adjacent` and give a clean positive substitute.
- Encode `forbidden` only in negative-control fields.

Safe replacement discipline:
When blocking or narrowing a JD phrase, provide a clean positive replacement if one exists.

Bad safe replacement examples:
- `adjacent-to-[target] work`
- `[target]-like responsibilities`
- `[unsupported system]-adjacent tooling`
- `[lifecycle/scope]-adjacent workflows`

Good safe replacement patterns:
- `[supported product category] for [supported customer/user context]`
- `[supported workflow] that addresses [JD-native problem]`
- `[supported capability] without implying [unsupported system]`
- `[source-native mechanism] reframed into [validated JD-native outcome]`
- clean product noun, not a legalistic caveat.

Generic examples by structure:
- Unsupported exact tool, supported workflow: use the workflow, block the tool.
- Unsupported customer type, supported user problem: use the user problem, block the customer type.
- Unsupported implementation ownership, supported product requirements: use product requirements/platform standardization, block hands-on engineering.
- Unsupported formal metric program, supported proxy metrics: use the proxy metric family, block formal program ownership.

Hard-stretch translation layer:
For the JD's 3-6 highest-value target-native phrases, decide the strongest safe tier:
1. exact JD phrase;
2. near-exact JD phrase with constrained scope;
3. clean target-native replacement phrase;
4. clean source-to-JD bridge phrase;
5. skill-only phrase;
6. blocked phrase.

The strongest safe tier should surface in title, summary, storylines, bullet instructions, or skills unless truly unsupported/forbidden.

Title/headline stress test:
Internally compare:
1. current/source-native title;
2. JD role title + strongest safe modifier;
3. boldest JD-native positioning title;
4. constrained JD-native title.

Choose the cleanest real-sounding title that maximizes recruiter and hiring-manager recognition without changing true seniority, employer, role family, or unsupported system ownership.

Title rules:
- `personalInfo.title` is a positioning headline, not an employment-title mutation.
- It may change the product/domain scope modifier when evidence supports it.
- Do not create awkward keyword mashups.
- If a central JD phrase is functionally equivalent and clean, include it rather than dropping it because a related subclaim is unsupported.
- Actual `workExperience[].title` must remain source-accurate in Prompt 3.

Summary rules for this brief:
- `summary_lead` must be a clean resume instruction, not a caveat.
- Lead with strongest JD-native domain identity and biggest supported proof.
- Avoid vague leadership language; seniority should show up through ownership/proof shape, not title inflation alone.
- Do not use hedge words or gap language.
- If an exact JD phrase is too risky, use the clean positive replacement, not a caveat-loaded phrase.

Skills rules for this brief:
- Skills must be recruiter-native labels, not legalistic defenses.
- If a skill requires the word `adjacent` to remain truthful, choose a cleaner safe replacement or omit it.
- Prefer strong supported nouns over padded coverage.
- Hard maximum 12 skills.

Chronology-preserving current-role compression:
- Preserve chronological/source order by default.
- If the current role is less relevant than a prior role, recommend compressing it to 1-2 bullets.
- Do not recommend moving it down unless the user explicitly enables relevance-first ordering.
- Make the most relevant prior role dominate through headline, summary, lead bullet, bullet order within role, and bullet strength.

Current-role compression default:
- If the current/most recent role is less relevant than a prior role, default to exactly 1 bullet instruction for that current role.
- Allow 2 bullets only when the second bullet adds direct or functionally equivalent target-domain proof, not merely general product quality, founder credibility, AI interest, or process excellence.
- When compressing to 1 bullet, combine the role's best supported result and mechanism into one strong Google XYZ bullet.

Compressed-current-role length rule:
- If the current role is less relevant and should be compressed to one bullet, one bullet must still be readable.
- The single bullet should usually contain one result, one proof metric, and one mechanism cluster.
- Do not turn one bullet into a packed paragraph.
- Prefer a bullet that would fit in roughly two resume lines in a normal resume layout.
- If multiple true mechanisms exist, choose the 1-2 most convincing mechanisms and omit the rest.
- Do not let chronology preservation consume top-of-page attention from the strongest relevant prior role.

Scope-bullet rule:
Use a scope-setting bullet when the JD hires for domain/platform/product/infrastructure/lifecycle/operating-model ownership or cross-functional scope.
Pattern:
`Led [clean JD-native product/domain scope] across [supported surfaces/workflows] for [accurate customer/user context] - [scale], [usage metric]`

Do not overload the scope bullet with product scope, user scale, usage scale, team size, direct reports, revenue, and architecture all at once. Prioritize product scope + scale. Team size belongs only if the JD values people leadership or if it materially improves credibility.

Bullet instruction rules:
- `primary_message` must be clean final-resume language, without markers or caveats.
- `primary_metric` should contain the one strongest metric or supported paired metric.
- `mechanism` should state what the candidate changed, built, prioritized, launched, standardized, deprecated, measured, packaged, sequenced, or tested.
- `optional_context` may contain `FUNCTIONALLY_EQUIVALENT:` defense and guardrails.
- `do_not_include` should block unsupported subclaims.
- Every instruction must give Prompt 3 XYZ materials: X result/claim, Y proof/metric/scope, Z mechanism.
- Keep the named concrete specifics from the source (named deployment surface, production/customer signal, specific metric, unusually specific detail) inside `primary_message`/`primary_metric`; do not abstract them into generic wrappers — Prompt 3 needs the specific to write concretely.

Hard XYZ example pattern:
- Some bullets have hidden XYZ that is easy to miss. Prompt 2 must expose it clearly for Prompt 3.
- Example by structure:
  - Weak instruction: `Scaled [product] to [customers] by defining requirements and rollout priorities.`
  - Strong instruction: `Launched a 0-to-1 [product/platform] to [customer count] in [timeframe] by sequencing rollout around the highest-risk/highest-value user actions first rather than lower-friction paths.`
  - X = launched/scaled the 0-to-1 product/platform.
  - Y = customer count, timeframe, usage, or adoption metric.
  - Z = the sequencing decision around the highest-risk/highest-value actions.
- Use this pattern generically; do not copy the example domain unless the JD and evidence support it.

Bullet instruction compression:
- `primary_message` should be the lead claim Prompt 3 can use, not a full final bullet.
- `primary_metric` should contain one metric cluster, or one clean paired metric cluster when inseparable.
- `mechanism` should include only the 1-2 mechanisms most needed to defend the claim.
- Do not write `if useful`, `optionally`, or multiple alternative metrics inside `primary_metric`.
- If several metrics are available, choose the one that best proves the hiring manager's core doubt.
- Prefer fewer, sharper bullet instructions over exhaustive instructions that make Prompt 3 concatenate too much.

Bullet clarity gate for instructions:
- Before finalizing a bullet instruction, read the likely final bullet as if you are the hiring manager.
- It should be easy to understand, straightforward, and convincing.
- If the instruction produces vague wording, product bingo, or a long list of activities, revise the instruction to a clearer causal product decision.
- Use simple words when a keyword is not needed for ATS or hiring-manager recognition.

Decision-mechanism rule:
- In bullet instructions, `mechanism` should name the PM-owned execution choice, product decision, lever, packaging choice, rollout choice, threshold, or constraint that caused the result.
- Express technical depth through PM attribution: identified signal gaps, defined thresholds/specs, set rollout criteria, packaged self-serve tooling, redesigned workflow, sequenced rollout, or balanced tradeoffs. Do not imply model training, coding, or infrastructure ownership unless the evidence explicitly supports it.
- For PM and senior PM targets, prefer bullets that prove shipped or operationalized execution first, then preserve diagnosis or tradeoff when it strengthens credibility.
- When compressing a detail-heavy proof point, preserve the strongest non-redundant source-supported anchors rather than collapsing the bullet to one generic idea.
- Usually keep one primary result/mechanism pair, plus one supporting diagnosis, tradeoff, constraint, or outcome anchor when it materially strengthens credibility for this JD.
- Drop lower-value specifics before dropping a distinct high-value anchor.
- If two details do the same job, keep the sharper and more JD-relevant one.
- Do not rewrite a concrete anchor into abstract umbrella nouns such as `capabilities`, `solutions`, `strategy`, or `delivery` when a sharper supported anchor exists.
- Do not flatten sharp outcomes or diagnoses into abstract evaluative phrasing when the source supports a more concrete unmet need, failure pattern, held-constant metric, adoption outcome, or business result.
- Include at least one falsifiable detail when available: named signal, threshold, failure mode, held-constant metric, or beneficiary bottleneck.
- Do not use work artifacts such as PRDs, API requirements, stakeholder alignment, roadmap ownership, or documentation as the primary mechanism unless the actual decision inside that artifact is named.
- Strong mechanism pattern: `sequenced [rollout/customer/workflow] around [highest-value constraint]`, `calibrated [threshold/policy/model/product rule] to balance [tradeoff]`, `standardized [platform/packaging/process] to reduce [cost/time/risk]`, `pivoted [roadmap/product direction] toward [emerging customer/risk/market need]`.
- Weak mechanism pattern: `defined PRDs`, `wrote requirements`, `managed stakeholders`, `led roadmap`, `owned execution`, `aligned teams`.
- Work artifacts may appear only as supporting context, not as the causal Z.

Causal-Z test:
- For every non-scope bullet instruction, Z must answer: `What product decision made X happen, and why would a hiring manager believe it caused Y?`
- Do not stop at activities, artifacts, or task lists. Convert the underlying product judgment into a causal phrase.
- Weak Z examples by structure: `defined requirements`, `set rollout priorities`, `refined flows`, `managed pilots`, `aligned stakeholders`, `worked with data science`, `improved rules`.
- Strong Z examples by structure: `sequenced rollout around [highest-risk/highest-value workflow] first`, `introduced [control/check] before [critical user/system step]`, `calibrated [rule/threshold/model] to balance [tradeoff]`, `pivoted [roadmap] toward [emerging risk/customer need]`, `standardized [packaging/platform/process] to remove [friction/cost/risk]`.
- If the source contains many actions, select the 1-2 actions most likely to convince the hiring manager that the result was caused by the candidate's product judgment.
- Write Z as a meaningful causal phrase, not a comma-separated activity list.
- Vary Z shape across a role's bullets when the evidence allows it; do not let every top bullet use the same bare `by [verb]-ing [noun]` mold.
- In the top 2-3 bullets of the most relevant role, prefer execution-heavy Z that still preserves the reason the move was right when support exists.
- For PM and senior PM targets, the strongest top bullets usually show the shipped change or operating choice first, then preserve diagnosis, insight, or tradeoff when it materially strengthens credibility.
- Do not force diagnosis-first framing when the strongest truthful proof is rollout sequencing, workflow redesign, threshold/rule tuning, packaging, self-serve tooling, or other operationalized execution.
- `rather than` is allowed when the rejected path is a close believable alternative implied by the source evidence or Prompt 2 instruction.
- If the alternative would feel invented or too speculative in an interview answer, use triggering insight, named constraint, or bare mechanism instead.
- It is acceptable for Z to be slightly longer when the extra words make the product decision more convincing.

Mechanism-list repair for instructions:
- If `mechanism` contains more than 3 comma-separated actions, rewrite it into the 1-2 product decisions that caused the result.
- Prefer causal decision phrases over activity inventories.
- Example structure:
  - weak: `optimized A, B, C, D, and E`
  - strong: `redesigned [workflow] and calibrated [tradeoff metric] to reduce [failure mode]`
- If a list is necessary because the list itself is the product strategy, name the strategy first, then keep only the most important components.
- Avoid mechanisms that read like raw implementation checklists.

Important-experience bullet budget:
- For the most relevant candidate-owned role, allow 4-6 bullet instructions when each bullet proves a distinct high-value signal.
- Treat all source bullets for the same role as one evidence pool, not as separate draft sets that each deserve output.
- Merge overlapping source bullets by achievement or proof theme, not by sentence wording.
- If multiple source bullets express the same underlying achievement with different phrasing, metrics, or mechanisms, keep one instruction for that achievement using the strongest truthful metric plus the strongest causal mechanism combination.
- Do not create separate bullet instructions for two versions of the same launch, same platform scale story, same revenue expansion, same cost reduction, or same conversion/fraud-improvement story unless they answer clearly different hiring-manager doubts.

High-signal proof preservation rule:
- If a high-strength `selected_storylines` entry contains a distinct role-winning proof point, it must map to at least one of:
  1. `summary_lead`;
  2. `excitement_anchor`;
  3. the first two bullets of the most relevant role;
  4. a dedicated `bullet_rewrite_instructions` entry.
- Do not assume Prompt 3 will infer a bullet from `selected_storylines` alone.
- If the most relevant role has 4-6 allowed bullets, preserve distinct proof categories such as:
  - launch/adoption;
  - performance/success metrics;
  - fraud/risk outcome;
  - scale/usage;
  - revenue or business growth;
  - integration/tooling acceleration;
  - operating leverage or cost/reliability improvement.
- Distinct proof categories means different hiring-manager doubts answered by the role, not different draft phrasings of the same achievement.
- When two source bullets share the same core X and Y but vary mostly in wording or supporting detail, merge them into one stronger bullet instruction rather than preserving both.
- Do not drop high-signal performance proof such as success rate, reliability, false rejection, fraud reduction, conversion, latency, cost, adoption, or usage when it answers a different hiring-manager doubt from the other bullets.
- When two proof points share the same product but answer different doubts, usually keep both if the role is the main fit engine. Example structure: one bullet can prove `launched/scaled product`; another can prove `improved success/reliability/fraud tradeoff`.
- If space is tight, remove lower-signal team/process bullets before removing distinct performance, risk, reliability, conversion, or fraud outcome proof.
- Do not compress the most relevant role below the proof density needed for the hiring manager.
- Less relevant current roles should still be compressed when appropriate.
- Older or weakly relevant roles should stay concise unless Prompt 2 has a specific high-value proof reason.

Competitiveness floor:
Before output, check whether the recommended title, summary lead, first selected storyline, and first scope-bullet instruction could describe many generic PMs. If yes, strengthen the domain scope, metric, or mechanism.

Technical skills calibration:
- In `top_skills`, keep technical terms when they are source-supported and the JD/hiring-manager persona is technical.
- Do not over-sanitize skills into generic business wording if the role values technical depth.
- For technical roles, prefer a balanced skill mix:
  - domain/product surface;
  - technical system or platform language;
  - metric/tradeoff language;
  - customer/business outcome language.
- For less technical roles, use plain-language skills first and move technical terms lower or omit them when they do not improve fit.

Story-bank expansion signal discipline:
- Do not create story-bank questions merely because a JD gap exists.
- Create gap questions only when a strong answer could materially improve the next resume through one of:
  1. `strengthen_existing_bullet`
  2. `summary_title_or_skill_unlock`
  3. `replace_existing_bullet`
  4. `new_bullet_candidate`
  5. `interview_prep_only`
- Prefer `strengthen_existing_bullet` over `new_bullet_candidate` when an existing high-priority bullet is directionally right but lacks a sharper metric, mechanism, customer/user detail, technical term, or product decision.
- Do not ask a `new_bullet_candidate` question unless the answer could plausibly become one of the top 4-6 bullets in the most relevant role or replace a weaker current bullet.
- If the most relevant role already has 6 strong bullets, any new-bullet question must name the current proof category it could replace.
- If the answer would only help interview defense and would not change resume text, tag it as `interview_prep_only` or omit it from top feedback.
- Do not let story-bank feedback needs weaken `recommended_title`, `summary_lead`, selected storylines, or bullet instructions.

Bullet-worthiness test:
- A resume-actionable question must be able to produce or improve XYZ:
  - X: outcome, launch, improvement, risk reduction, adoption, decision, or product result;
  - Y: metric, scale, timeframe, user/customer, risk, revenue, conversion, reliability, latency, or operational proof;
  - Z: product decision, mechanism, tradeoff, sequencing choice, workflow change, platform choice, policy/rule change, or operating model.
- If likely answers would lack Y or Z, do not ask the question as a resume-expansion item.
- If the missing proof would only help defend existing wording in interviews, classify it as `interview_prep_only`.

Question-slot prioritization:
- Provide at most 3-4 top gap questions.
- Rank by expected impact on the final resume:
  1. strengthens an existing first-screen or most-relevant-role bullet;
  2. unlocks stronger title, summary, or top skill wording;
  3. could replace the weakest current bullet with stronger JD-native proof;
  4. could become a new bullet only if a slot exists or it clearly beats an existing bullet;
  5. interview-prep-only.
- Use only the top questions that can change the next resume. Omit low-impact or purely interesting questions.
- For each top gap question, include the intended resume action inside the existing `gaps` string:
  `[gap_type=<hard_gate|near_gate|differentiator|interview_prep> | impact=<high|medium|low> | action=<must_add_if_true|safer_positioning|optional_storybank_add|interview_prep_only> | resume_action=<strengthen_existing_bullet|summary_title_or_skill_unlock|replace_existing_bullet|new_bullet_candidate|interview_prep_only> | target=<role_or_bullet_theme>]`
- After the tag, phrase the gap as a concrete question the user can answer with a STAR story or a detail update to an existing STAR story.
- Good question pattern for strengthening a bullet:
  `Would strengthen the existing [bullet theme] bullet: can you provide [missing metric/mechanism/customer/technical detail] so the bullet can show [X/Y/Z improvement]?`
- Good question pattern for a replacement/new bullet:
  `Could replace [current lower-priority proof category] only if strong: do you have a STAR story where you [capability], for [user/customer], by [decision/mechanism], resulting in [metric/outcome]?`
- Good question pattern for title/summary/skill unlock:
  `Would unlock stronger [title/summary/skill] wording: do you have proof of [capability] with [scope/mechanism/metric]?`

Gap severity calibration:
- Use `hard_gate` only when the JD explicitly requires the capability, the role cannot credibly be performed without it, and the candidate lacks a functional substitute.
- Use `near_gate` when the capability is central to hiring-manager confidence but adjacent proof may still earn an interview.
- Use `differentiator` when the evidence would materially improve competitiveness but is not required for recruiter or hiring-manager pass-through.
- Use `interview_prep` when the topic is useful for interviews but should not drive resume positioning.
- Use `must_add_if_true` when the resume should include the story if the candidate truly has it.
- Use `safer_positioning` when missing proof should cap the title, summary, scope bullet, or skills away from the most niche JD-native claim.
- Use `optional_storybank_add` when the story would strengthen the resume but should not block applying.
- Use `interview_prep_only` when the story should be prepared for interviews but not added to the resume.
- Do not overuse `hard_gate`; most useful feedback questions are `near_gate` or `differentiator`.
- Do not mark a broad JD-native phrase as `hard_gate` merely because some exact sub-surfaces are missing.
- If competitive-stretch acceptance supports the broader phrase, use `gaps` to identify bullet-strengthening, replacement, or interview-defense needs rather than forcing safer resume positioning.

Examples of tagged gaps:
- `[gap_type=near_gate | impact=high | action=must_add_if_true | resume_action=strengthen_existing_bullet | target=payment-authentication success bullet] Would strengthen the existing bullet: can you provide the 1-2 product decisions, threshold/rule changes, or workflow controls that most directly caused the metric improvement?`
- `[gap_type=differentiator | impact=medium | action=optional_storybank_add | resume_action=replace_existing_bullet | target=weakest lower-priority bullet] Could replace a weaker bullet only if strong: do you have a candidate-owned story showing [capability], [decision/mechanism], [customer/user], and [metric/outcome]?`
- `[gap_type=interview_prep | impact=low | action=interview_prep_only | resume_action=interview_prep_only | target=interview defense] Prepare an interview explanation of [boundary/unsupported surface] without forcing it into the resume.`
- `[gap_type=differentiator | impact=high | action=optional_storybank_add | resume_action=summary_title_or_skill_unlock | target=seniority scope] Would unlock positioning at the level this JD implies: do you have a candidate-owned story showing cross-team, multi-product, platform, or org-level scope (teams influenced, products owned, scale) with concrete proof?`

Final context-leak audit:
- Remove any company-wide metric from proof_points, summary_lead, excitement_anchor, and bullet instructions unless it appears in candidate-owned bullet/project evidence.
- Remove broad company product lines from bullets unless the candidate-owned source bullet says the candidate owned that product line.
- If the current resume has a company context field, preserve it as context only; do not mine it for bullet facts.
- If you need a scale metric, prefer candidate-owned metrics from role descriptions over employer-wide context metrics.

Final audit before output:

- Technical language is calibrated to the JD and hiring-manager persona: preserve source-supported signal jargon for technical roles; translate noise jargon for less technical roles.
- Original resume technical terms are not stripped when they improve credibility for a technical PM, platform, AI/ML, risk, fraud, security, identity, infra, API, or fintech role.
- Bullets remain understandable even when technical terms are preserved.

- Every high-strength selected storyline has a visible execution path in summary, excitement anchor, first relevant bullets, or a dedicated bullet instruction.
- Distinct proof categories in the most relevant role are preserved when 4-6 bullets are allowed; do not drop performance/success metrics just because launch/scale is already represented.
- `summary_lead` includes the most differentiated proof from `excitement_anchor` when placement is `summary` or `both`.
