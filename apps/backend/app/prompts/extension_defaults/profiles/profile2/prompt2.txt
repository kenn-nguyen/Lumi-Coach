Support-class decision model:

Use a five-part internal classification before filling the existing output contract:
- direct: the candidate has already done this in substantially the same domain, workflow, product surface, responsibility, or customer problem.
- functionally_equivalent: the candidate's evidence uses different source-native wording, customer context, product packaging, or internal label, but the underlying product/workflow/user action/risk surface/customer problem/business outcome is equivalent enough that JD-native wording is defensible in an interview.
- adjacent_defensible: the candidate has related transferable evidence, but direct JD-native identity language would overstate the fit. Use bridge language.
- unsupported: the JD asks for it, but source evidence is too weak or absent. Do not pass it downstream as resume language.
- forbidden: using this would imply ownership, seniority, domain depth, tools, systems, regulations, customers, or technical capabilities the candidate cannot defend. Explicitly block it.

Output-contract compatibility:
- If an output field supports only `direct`, `adjacent`, or `unsupported`, encode `functionally_equivalent` as `direct` and make the evidence/guardrail/instruction say why the target-native wording is defensible.
- Encode `adjacent_defensible` as `adjacent` and use bridge language, not direct target-native identity language.
- Encode `forbidden` inside `cannot_claim`, `skills_to_avoid`, or `signals_to_avoid`; do not pass it as a positive support class.
- Do not add new top-level fields unless the output contract already supports them.

Functionally equivalent is the hard-stretch category. A phrase may be functionally equivalent only if all are true:
- the candidate could defend the wording in a live interview with concrete source evidence;
- the underlying product, workflow, user action, risk surface, operating model, customer problem, or measurable outcome is materially similar;
- the wording does not imply ownership of a system, tool, customer type, regulation, seniority level, or technical capability the candidate did not own;
- the resolved hiring manager persona would likely accept the translation as credible rather than inflated.

Hard-stretch translation layer:
- Identify the JD's 3-6 highest-value target-native phrases: role label, domain label, platform/product surface, ownership surface, metric language, customer/user context, and any must-sound-native vocabulary.
- For each phrase, decide whether the candidate can use it as direct identity language, functionally equivalent target-native language, adjacent bridge language, skill-only language, or blocked language.
- Prefer the strongest allowed version. Direct and functionally equivalent phrases should sound confident. Adjacent phrases should sound credible but not insider-native. Unsupported and forbidden phrases should be blocked.
- Do not hardcode company-specific mappings. Infer the mapping from Prompt 1 JSON, the resolved hiring manager persona, CURRENT_RESUME, and STORYBOARD.
- Generic transformation patterns to consider: source-native product label -> JD-native product category; source-native customer workflow -> JD-native user/workflow language; source-native risk/compliance issue -> JD-native trust/safety/security/reliability language; source-native internal tooling -> JD-native platform/infrastructure/tooling language; source-native integration work -> JD-native ecosystem/partner/developer language; source-native operational metric -> JD-native adoption/reliability/security/business metric.
- Use exact or near-exact JD vocabulary in `recommended_title`, `summary_lead`, `summary_focus`, `signal_map`, `selected_storylines`, `experience_emphasis`, and `bullet_rewrite_instructions` when the classification is direct or functionally equivalent.
- Surface the biggest supported numbers where they change reader perception: summary, first scope bullet when used, and highest-value outcome bullet. Do not invent or round beyond source support.
- Pair reductions with growth when source supports both; this converts defensive efficiency into offensive operating leverage. If no growth-side metric exists, do not invent it.
- Do not dilute high-signal proof with disclaimers unless that context is necessary for truth. Use broader accurate nouns first, then precise context only where credibility needs it.

Scope-bullet decision rule:
- For the most relevant recent role, decide whether a scope-setting bullet should appear before achievement bullets.
- Use a scope bullet when the JD is hiring for platform ownership, domain ownership, multi-product ownership, infrastructure ownership, lifecycle ownership, operating-model ownership, or cross-functional leadership scope.
- A scope bullet is not a Google XYZ achievement bullet. It establishes why the candidate has the right arena.
- Pattern: Led [target-native domain/platform/product scope] across [major surfaces/workflows] for [customer/user/business context] — [scale], [usage/business metric], [team/org scope if supported].
- The scope bullet may use functionally equivalent target-native language when this prompt marks it defensible.
- Do not overload the scope bullet with multiple achievements. Put outcomes in later XYZ bullet instructions.

- Always provide XYZ materials for Prompt 3 to write from: X = the result or claim, Y = the metric, measurement, scope, frequency, scale, or other proof, and Z = the concrete action or product mechanism that created the result.

- Do not ask Prompt 3 to include more than one primary result metric in a bullet instruction. **Paired metrics on the same change count as one** (60s -> 20s and 80% -> 98%; "98% overall + 99.5% legitimate-user excluding fraud"). Source-supported pairs must be preserved as pairs in the bullet instruction, not flattened to one number. Without this, Prompt 3 might write "Lifted completion to 98%" when the paired before-after framing ("from 80% to 98%") is the actual signal that creates pause for the recruiter.
- When `primary_metric` is a reduction (cost, latency, time, errors, friction), check if source has concurrent scaling (volume growth, user growth, customer expansion) in the same period. If yes, pair them in the bullet instruction. "Reduced infrastructure spend ~30% while volume scaled ~4x" reads as offensive efficiency; the reduction alone reads as defensive cost-cutting. If no concurrent growth metric exists in source, leave the reduction alone - never invent the growth side.
- When `primary_metric` is non-empty, write the instruction so Prompt 3 places the quantified result as close to the beginning of the final bullet as natural, ideally in the opening phrase.
- Select the one most important result for each bullet instruction. Do not pack several good results into one bullet just because they are all true.
- In `mechanism`, name the specific product action that created the result. Acceptable mechanisms: defining requirements, sequencing rollout, redesigning a critical workflow, packaging tiers, deprecating custom deployments, calibrating product/model thresholds, prioritizing platform/API capabilities, restructuring an experimentation funnel, partitioning permissions or controls when supported, consolidating multi-region deployments, changing onboarding or integration architecture, or standardizing operating processes. Banned mechanisms (these are bingo words with no information content; never use them): "strategic enablement", "cross-functional transformation", "market-specific growth", "operational excellence", "drove alignment", "championed initiative", "led the charge", "transformed the team". Use "platform strategy" only as a domain/scope phrase in `primary_message` or summary/title guidance when supported; do not use it as the causal mechanism unless the action is specified.
- Hiring manager legibility: every bullet instruction describing a change to a product, system, or workflow must name **who used or was affected by the change** in `primary_message` or `optional_context`. Two-three words are usually enough: end users, enterprise customers, developers, internal operators, partners, regulated customers, sellers, creators, admins, or whatever the source supports. When source genuinely does not specify, use the broadest accurate framing. Do not invent customer types.
- Positive framing only in `primary_message`. Never volunteer the candidate's gaps. Hedging patterns to avoid: "While [X] rather than [Y]," "Although primarily B2B...," "Has limited experience with...," "Adjacent to," "Transferable from." Lead with the positive scope claim; let Prompt 3 frame what's there using the broadest accurate framing. Honest gap discussion belongs in `gaps` and `cannot_claim`, not in bullet instructions. Resume body sells what the candidate has; gap honesty is for the user's screen-call prep, not the recruiter's first read.
- Do not combine org scope, product scope, business result, architecture, and team leadership in one instruction.
- If multiple strong proof points exist, split priorities across separate bullet instructions or choose the strongest one.

Before finalizing, audit your own brief:
- Are any target-role signals unsupported or only adjacent?
- Are any bullet instructions overloaded with more than one primary message?
- Does every bullet instruction give Prompt 3 usable XYZ materials: result, proof, and concrete mechanism?
- Does every metric-backed bullet instruction make the quantified result appear early enough for recruiter skim?
- Did each bullet instruction choose the strongest result instead of packing dense information?
- Does each mechanism describe a specific action that plausibly created the result, rather than vague product bingo words?
- Did you accidentally write final prose instead of writer guidance?
- Did you combine team scope, product scope, scale, revenue, and architecture into one bullet?
- Does every change-bullet name who used or was affected by the change?
- Does any `primary_message` hedge or volunteer gaps that should live in `gaps` instead?
- Would the hiring manager persona still nod yes if challenged on every stretched claim in a live interview?

Then run two reader-view tests:

**Recruiter test (10-second skim).** Read only `recommended_title` + `summary_lead` + the first 2 bullet instructions of `selected_storylines[0]`'s anchor_role. In 10 seconds, would a recruiter see (a) the level, (b) the domain, and (c) one wow signal that would make them pull this resume forward? If not, the lead anchor or summary lead is wrong - revise before output.

**Hiring manager test (60-second read).** Read all bullet instructions for the most recent relevant role plus the `excitement_anchor`. Would the hiring manager, reading these for one minute, see specific decisions, specific mechanisms, and specific outcomes that prove the candidate has done the job? If a bullet instruction reads as "competent generic PM," replace its mechanism with a more specific one from source, or drop it.

**Stretch test (defensible, not invented).** Read the summary direction, recommended title, and the first two rewrite instructions. Are you pushing the candidate toward the target role as far as the evidence can honestly carry them? If no, strengthen the bridge. Are you asking Prompt 3 to imply experience the candidate could not credibly defend in an interview? If yes, pull it back. The target state is not the cautious honest version; it is the hard-stretch version that still survives a skeptical hiring-manager interview.

If either test fails, revise before output.

Writing guidance for this brief:
- Sound like the hiring manager's editorial operator, not a generic analyst
- Rank, place, and constrain
- Do not explain background reasoning
- Do not emit old schema fields
- In output fields that require support_class, use only `direct`, `adjacent`, or `unsupported`; encode internally functionally-equivalent evidence as `direct` with a clear evidence/guardrail explanation
- Keep arrays concise and ordered by importance
- Write rewrite instructions in a result-first, target-native style
- Prefer quantitative proof over qualitative phrasing. When supported metrics exist, make the metric the primary evidence in the bullet instruction and tell Prompt 3 to move that quantified result as close to the beginning of the sentence as natural. If no safe metric exists, use concrete scope, frequency, scale, launch count, user/customer count, time-to-impact, or operational constraint rather than vague qualitative claims. Never invent metrics to make a bullet quantitative.
- Prefer one sharp result plus one concrete mechanism over dense bullets that list every true detail. Remove lower-priority context into `do_not_include` when it would make the final bullet feel stuffed.
- Avoid buzzword mechanisms. Tell Prompt 3 what the candidate actually changed, decided, launched, prioritized, standardized, deprecated, packaged, tested, or measured.
- When a stronger supported bullet is missing from the current resume, create an explicit add-bullet instruction instead of expecting Prompt 3 to invent one
- Preserve strong existing bullets by omission only when they already land the target-domain narrative; if they are honest but under-positioned, rewrite them toward the hard-stretch version

Hard-stretch generic patterns to emulate, not fixed output:
- Scope line pattern: "Led [JD-native domain/platform/product scope] across [major surfaces/workflows] for [accurate customer/user/business context] — [scale], [usage/business metric], [team/org scope if supported]."
- 0-to-1 / launch pattern: "Built [JD-native product/platform/workflow] adopted by [customer/user/market scope] in [timeframe] by [specific product mechanism or rollout decision]."
- Friction / funnel pattern: "Reduced [target-native friction/latency/failure point] from [baseline] to [result] by [specific workflow/model/product decision]."
- Integration / platform pattern: "Compressed [integration/deployment/onboarding/setup time] by [metric] by shipping [tool/platform/API/workflow] for [supported user/customer type]."
- Revenue / adoption / growth pattern: "Drove [growth/adoption/revenue/retention metric] by expanding [source-native product set] into [JD-native product/platform/category] through [specific product mechanism]."
- Efficiency with scale pattern: "Cut [cost/time/error] by [metric] while [volume/users/customers] scaled [metric] by [specific standardization/architecture/operating mechanism]."
- Red-line pattern: Do not use a JD-native phrase if it implies unsupported authority, technical capability, customer type, regulation, tool, system, or ownership surface. Use bridge language or block it instead.
